# Real Estate Bazaar — Marketplace Domain & Boundary Model

**Decision date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** Approved product baseline; the foundational domain doc that the tenant/authorization architecture sits underneath.
**Relates to:** `tenant-view-model-architecture.md` (tenant authorization layer), `tenant-authorization-architecture.md` (tenant data model)
**Scope:** What Real Estate Bazaar IS as a product — the marketplace entities, actors, ownership layers, transaction flows, and cross-tenant relationships that exist independently of any single tenant.

---

## 0. The one-sentence positioning

> **Real Estate Bazaar is a real-estate marketplace. Agencies and agents operate within it; consumers transact through it; the platform owns the marketplace-level truths that outlive any single tenant.**

Everything below elaborates this sentence. The tenant/authorization docs describe HOW agencies+agents work; this doc describes WHAT the product actually is.

---

## 1. Why this document exists

The prior tenant/interaction architecture docs correctly modeled the agency+agent authorization layer, but treated `tenant` as if it were the fundamental product concept. It isn't. RB is a marketplace competing with Property Finder and Zillow — a multi-sided platform with consumers, agencies, agents, listings, properties, and platform-owned transactions.

The following entities exist in the product but were not modeled in the tenant docs:

- **Consumers** (buyers, renters, sellers, browsers) — actors with no agency membership
- **Properties** (the real-world assets) — durable across representations
- **Publications** (the public-marketplace view of properties) — platform-owned, spanning agencies
- **Marketplace-generated Leads** — created by consumer action, not agent action
- **Cross-tenant Transactions** — a Cedar-represented buyer purchasing an Elite-owned listing is a routine, not an exception
- **Multi-party Commission ledgers** — platform-owned, cross-agency by design
- **Representation relationships** — buyer-side, listing-side, referral, mandate — distinct from tenant membership

Without these primitives, the tenant doc kept treating marketplace-normal behavior as authorization violations. The result was a beautiful agency CRM that couldn't answer "who owns the transaction when Cedar's buyer purchases Elite's listing?"

This doc fixes that gap. The tenant doc is repositioned as ONE layer (the security/organizational layer) inside the broader architecture defined here.

## 2. The three ownership layers

Every entity in Real Estate Bazaar belongs to exactly one of three ownership layers. The layer determines who can access, modify, or dispose of the entity.

### Layer 1 — Platform (RB itself)

Entities that outlive any tenant and describe marketplace-level truth:

- **Property** — the durable real-world entity (an apartment in Achrafieh)
- **Publication** — the public-marketplace aggregated view of a Property
- **Consumer / Platform User** — buyers/renters/browsers with no agency membership
- **Person** — global identity resolved across consumers, agent contacts, and transaction participants
- **Marketplace Lead** — a consumer inquiry generated by clicking "Contact Agent"
- **Viewing** — a scheduled property viewing (owned by platform, references agency + consumer)
- **Offer** — a formal purchase/lease offer (platform-recorded, cross-agency-capable)
- **Transaction** — a closed deal, immutable financial record
- **Commission Ledger** — multi-party ledger against a Transaction
- **Marketplace Analytics** — platform-level metrics (visible to platform admin only)

Platform entities are governed by platform policy, not any agency's engagement policy. They exist regardless of which tenants participate.

### Layer 2 — Organizational (Agency)

Entities owned by a specific agency tenant:

- **Listing** — an agency's representation of a Property on the marketplace
- **Agency Contact** — an agency's CRM record of a Person
- **Agency Campaign** — an agency's marketing campaign
- **Agency Conversation** — messaging threads owned by the agency
- **Agency Channel Account** — the agency's WhatsApp Business, SMS sender, email domain
- **Agency Wallet** — the agency's billing pool
- **Agency Template** — reusable message/document templates
- **Agency Staff** — memberships attaching users to the agency
- **Agency Routing Policy** — the agency's lead-routing configuration
- **Agency Departure Policy** — the agency's rules on what departing agents keep/lose
- **Agency Engagement Policy** — the agency's rules on what members can do (per tenant doc §8)

Governed by the tenant model. `tenant_id` on each entity establishes ownership. Editable via the tenant model's rules. Cross-agency operations forbidden by default (per tenant doc §5).

### Layer 3 — Relationship (Agent + Person + Tenant + Time)

Entities that describe a relationship among multiple parties, potentially crossing agencies:

- **Buyer Representation** — a Person is represented by an Agent (in a Tenant) as buyer
- **Seller/Landlord Mandate** — an Agent (in a Tenant) is mandated to represent a seller
- **Referral** — one Tenant refers a Lead or Listing to another
- **Prior Affinity** — a Person has historical relationship with an Agent (may inform routing preferences)
- **Marketplace Transaction Participation** — Agent's role in a specific Transaction (buyer's rep, listing agent, referral source, etc.)
- **Commission Allocation** — a specific Agent's or Tenant's share in a specific Transaction's commission ledger

Relationships are inherently multi-tenant. Their authorization model is DIFFERENT from tenant-owned entities: instead of "which tenant owns this?", the question is "who is participating, in what role, with what agreement?"

## 3. Marketplace entities in detail

### 3.1 Property

The durable real-world entity. One Property = one physical thing in the world.

```sql
CREATE TABLE properties (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  external_ref TEXT UNIQUE,               -- MOF cadastral reference, if known
  address_normalized TEXT NOT NULL,       -- canonicalized address
  geo GEOGRAPHY(POINT, 4326),             -- PostGIS point
  property_type TEXT NOT NULL,            -- apartment / villa / land / commercial / etc
  characteristics JSONB NOT NULL,         -- beds, baths, area_sqm, year_built, etc
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active'
    CHECK (status IN ('active', 'sold', 'off_market', 'demolished')),
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  data JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb
);
CREATE INDEX idx_properties_geo ON properties USING GIST (geo);
```

**Critical:** `properties` has NO `tenant_id`. Properties are marketplace-level. Elite and Cedar can both list Property #123. Neither "owns" the underlying property; they own their Listings of it.

Property identity resolution uses cadastral reference (MOF Order of Engineers / Ministry of Finance reference where available), geocoded address, and characteristic-hash deduplication. Two agencies listing the same physical apartment → the platform recognizes it as one Property with two Listings.

### 3.2 Listing

An agency's representation of a Property on the marketplace. This IS the tenant-owned entity (per v2 tenant doc Principle 1).

```sql
CREATE TABLE listings (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  property_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES properties(id),
  tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),    -- owning agency
  created_by_user_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users(id),
  source_tenant_id TEXT REFERENCES tenants(id),       -- if referred from another agency
  tenant_assignment_source TEXT NOT NULL,
  responsible_tenant_id TEXT REFERENCES tenants(id),  -- delegated management, if different
  billing_tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),
  listing_agent_id TEXT REFERENCES users(id),         -- the individual agent
  representation_type TEXT NOT NULL                   -- exclusive_mandate / non_exclusive_mandate / marketing_agent / open_agency
    CHECK (representation_type IN ('exclusive_mandate', 'non_exclusive_mandate', 'marketing_agent', 'open_agency')),
  listing_price NUMERIC(14,2),
  currency TEXT NOT NULL,                             -- USD / LBP
  terms JSONB,                                        -- lease vs sale, terms, exclusivity dates
  publication_status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'draft'
    CHECK (publication_status IN ('draft', 'published', 'paused', 'withdrawn', 'expired', 'sold')),
  published_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  expires_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  data JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb
);
CREATE INDEX idx_listings_property ON listings(property_id);
CREATE INDEX idx_listings_tenant ON listings(tenant_id);
CREATE INDEX idx_listings_publication ON listings(publication_status) WHERE publication_status = 'published';
```

**Multiple listings for the same Property are legal** (non-exclusive marketing, open agency, marketplace competition). Each Listing carries its own agency's brand, price, and terms.

**Publication vs Ownership:** `listing.tenant_id` = owning agency (billing, control). `listing.publication_status` = whether it appears on the public marketplace feed. The public feed queries `WHERE publication_status = 'published'` and ignores tenant filter. Agency dashboards filter by tenant AND publication_status appropriately.

### 3.3 Publication

The public-marketplace aggregated view of a Property. When Consumers browse RB, they see Publications, not raw Listings.

```sql
CREATE TABLE publications (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  property_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES properties(id) UNIQUE,
  aggregated_listing_ids JSONB NOT NULL,     -- array of listings.id currently published
  primary_listing_id TEXT REFERENCES listings(id),  -- highest-quality/lowest-price/exclusive, per rule
  publication_state TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active'
    CHECK (publication_state IN ('active', 'hidden', 'moderation_hold', 'legal_hold')),
  first_published_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  marketplace_metadata JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb   -- score, tags, moderation flags
);
```

- **Aggregation:** if Elite and Cedar both list Property #123, the Publication shows a single property card with both listings underneath; the primary listing is chosen by policy (exclusive-mandate wins, else lowest price, else most-recent).
- **Marketplace state independent of listing state:** a Property can be hidden platform-wide (moderation, legal) while individual Listings remain `published`.
- **Consumers interact with Publications;** the platform routes their actions to the appropriate Listing(s).

### 3.4 Consumer / Platform User

Non-agent users of the marketplace: buyers, renters, sellers-posting-FSBO, browsers.

```sql
CREATE TABLE platform_users (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  person_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES persons(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  email TEXT UNIQUE,
  phone TEXT,
  password_hash TEXT,
  user_type TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'consumer'
    CHECK (user_type IN ('consumer', 'agent_referral', 'anonymous_lead')),
  registration_state TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active'
    CHECK (registration_state IN ('anonymous', 'registered', 'suspended', 'closed')),
  saved_searches JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,
  saved_properties JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,
  notification_preferences JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb,
  consent_state JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb,  -- cookies, marketing, analytics
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  last_active_at TIMESTAMPTZ
);
```

- **Anonymous browsers:** identified by session cookie (with consent). No `platform_users` row yet.
- **Registered consumers:** creating a `platform_users` row triggers a `persons` row (§3.5).
- **Consumers have NO tenant membership.** They interact with the marketplace and inquire on listings; they never "belong" to Elite or Cedar.
- **Agent-created contacts vs consumer-registered users are distinct:** Ali adding "Nadine" to his personal CRM does NOT auto-create a `platform_users` row for Nadine. Both records may point to the same `person_id` if identity is resolved, but Nadine has independent agency over her own registration.

### 3.5 Person (global identity)

Platform-level identity. One `persons` row per real human being.

```sql
CREATE TABLE persons (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  lifecycle_state TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active'
    CHECK (lifecycle_state IN ('active', 'deletion_requested', 'deleted_erased', 'deleted_anonymized', 'retained_for_legal')),
  lifecycle_state_changed_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  updated_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  data JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb
);

CREATE TABLE person_identifiers (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  person_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES persons(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  identifier_type TEXT NOT NULL              -- 'email' / 'phone_e164' / 'lebanese_id' / 'passport'
    CHECK (identifier_type IN ('email', 'phone_e164', 'lebanese_id', 'passport', 'other')),
  identifier_value_normalized TEXT NOT NULL,  -- lowercased email; E.164 phone; hashed for sensitive types
  verification_status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'unverified'
    CHECK (verification_status IN ('unverified', 'verified_otp', 'verified_document', 'verified_third_party')),
  verified_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  UNIQUE (identifier_type, identifier_value_normalized)
);
CREATE INDEX idx_person_identifiers_lookup ON person_identifiers(identifier_type, identifier_value_normalized);
```

**Global identity is separate from tenant-scoped data access:**

- The `persons` table knows Nadine exists as one real person
- Elite's `contacts` record for Nadine has `person_id = nadine_global_id`, tenant-scoped to Elite
- Cedar's `contacts` record for Nadine has the same `person_id`, tenant-scoped to Cedar
- Consumer registration for Nadine has the same `person_id`, in `platform_users`
- **None of these share data by default.** Each tenant's contact record is private to that tenant. Identity linkage is invisible unless explicit consent per tenant doc §7.

### 3.6 Deterministic identity resolution

Identity is resolved algorithmically + verifiably. The rules:

**High-confidence signals (auto-link on match):**
- Verified email (OTP-confirmed): canonical form (lowercased, RFC-normalized) match
- Verified phone (OTP-confirmed): E.164-normalized match. **Lebanese normalization rule:** `03-123-456` and `+961-3-123-456` and `03123456` all normalize to `+96103123456`; the same rule applied consistently to `+961 70`, `+961 71`, `+961 76`, `+961 78`, `+961 79` mobile prefixes and landline `+961 1` through `+961 9` prefixes. Local-format `0` prefix stripped and replaced with `+961`.
- Verified Lebanese ID (KYC-confirmed for high-value transactions): direct match on national identifier

**Medium-confidence signals (propose match, require confirmation):**
- Unverified email match — one side verified, other unverified
- Fuzzy phone match — matches after normalization but no OTP verification
- Fuzzy name + partial contact match — algorithmic proposal

**Never auto-links without at least one high-confidence signal.**

Every match records:
- `match_source` (verified_phone / verified_email / manual / algorithmic)
- `match_confidence` (0.0-1.0 for algorithmic)
- `match_status` (proposed / confirmed / rejected / withdrawn)

**Identity resolution and data access are separate:** the platform can know Elite's contact and Cedar's contact refer to the same Person without allowing either party to see the other's data. Cross-tenant data merging requires explicit consent (tenant doc §7).

### 3.7 Marketplace Lead

Generated when a Consumer performs a commercial-intent action on the marketplace (clicks "Contact Agent", requests a viewing, saves-and-inquires).

```sql
CREATE TABLE marketplace_leads (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  publication_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES publications(id),
  primary_listing_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES listings(id),  -- the specific listing being inquired about
  consumer_person_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES persons(id),
  receiving_tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),  -- auto-attributed: primary_listing.tenant_id
  lead_source TEXT NOT NULL                                   -- 'marketplace_contact_button' / 'marketplace_viewing_request' / 'marketplace_saved_inquiry' / 'external_referral'
    CHECK (lead_source IN ('marketplace_contact_button', 'marketplace_viewing_request', 'marketplace_saved_inquiry', 'external_referral', 'agent_created')),
  buyer_representation_context TEXT REFERENCES tenant_memberships(id),  -- consumer's active buyer rep if any
  routing_status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'pending'
    CHECK (routing_status IN ('pending', 'offered', 'claimed', 'assigned', 'timed_out', 'escalated', 'closed')),
  assigned_to_user_id TEXT REFERENCES users(id),
  claimed_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  first_response_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  claim_sla_deadline TIMESTAMPTZ,
  response_sla_deadline TIMESTAMPTZ,
  utm_source TEXT,
  utm_campaign TEXT,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  data JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'::jsonb
);
```

**Attribution is deterministic:** `receiving_tenant_id = primary_listing.tenant_id`. The agent never chooses. Pattern A/B/C from the tenant doc do not apply to marketplace-generated leads — the tenant is inherited from the listing being inquired about.

**Buyer representation influences routing but not ownership:** if the consumer has an active exclusive buyer representation with Cedar, and inquires on an Elite listing, the Lead is still attributed to Elite (listing's tenant), but Cedar's buyer-rep gets a notification and first-right-to-respond per platform's buyer-representation-first routing rule.

**Non-marketplace leads:** agent-created leads (Ali manually adds Nadine to his CRM via the agent dashboard) still exist as `marketplace_leads` rows with `lead_source = 'agent_created'`, but the tenant attribution follows the agent-creation flow per tenant doc §11 (explicit choice).

### 3.8 Viewing

A scheduled property viewing.

```sql
CREATE TABLE viewings (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  property_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES properties(id),
  listing_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES listings(id),
  consumer_person_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES persons(id),
  owning_tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),      -- listing's tenant
  arranging_tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),   -- may differ if buyer's rep arranged
  viewing_agent_id TEXT REFERENCES users(id),                 -- who's showing the property
  scheduled_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'scheduled'
    CHECK (status IN ('scheduled', 'confirmed', 'completed', 'cancelled_consumer', 'cancelled_agent', 'no_show')),
  outcome_notes TEXT,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

`owning_tenant_id` and `arranging_tenant_id` may differ: Cedar's buyer's-rep agent arranges a viewing on Elite's listing. Both tenants have a legitimate stake in the record. Data access follows tenant-scoping per participation.

### 3.9 Offer

A formal purchase or lease offer.

```sql
CREATE TABLE offers (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  listing_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES listings(id),
  offering_person_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES persons(id),
  offering_tenant_id TEXT REFERENCES tenants(id),  -- if offer submitted via an agency; NULL if direct consumer
  offer_amount NUMERIC(14,2) NOT NULL,
  currency TEXT NOT NULL,
  terms JSONB NOT NULL,
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'submitted'
    CHECK (status IN ('draft', 'submitted', 'countered', 'accepted', 'rejected', 'expired', 'withdrawn')),
  parent_offer_id TEXT REFERENCES offers(id),  -- for counters
  submitted_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  responded_at TIMESTAMPTZ
);
```

**Cross-tenant is normal here.** Cedar's buyer-rep submits an offer on Elite's listing: `offering_tenant_id = cedar`, `listing.tenant_id = elite`. Not a violation; a marketplace transaction.

### 3.10 Transaction

The closed deal. Platform-owned, immutable financial record.

```sql
CREATE TABLE marketplace_transactions (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  property_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES properties(id),
  listing_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES listings(id),
  accepted_offer_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES offers(id) UNIQUE,
  buyer_person_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES persons(id),
  seller_person_id TEXT REFERENCES persons(id),
  close_price NUMERIC(14,2) NOT NULL,
  currency TEXT NOT NULL,
  close_date DATE NOT NULL,
  transaction_type TEXT NOT NULL              -- 'sale' / 'lease' / 'rental_short_term' / etc
    CHECK (transaction_type IN ('sale', 'lease_long', 'lease_short', 'other')),
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'closed'
    CHECK (status IN ('pending_close', 'closed', 'reversed')),
  participating_tenant_ids JSONB NOT NULL,   -- array of tenant_ids
  participating_agent_ids JSONB NOT NULL,    -- array of user_ids with their role snapshot
  external_reference TEXT,                    -- notary/legal reference if applicable
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
```

**Transaction is platform-owned.** Neither Elite nor Cedar "owns" the transaction; both participate. Data access: each participating tenant sees the transaction record scoped to its own participation (its commission, its participating agents).

**Immutability:** transactions do not update. Corrections happen as `reversed` + new record.

### 3.11 Commission ledger

Multi-party ledger against a Transaction.

```sql
CREATE TABLE commission_ledger_entries (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  transaction_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES marketplace_transactions(id),
  recipient_tenant_id TEXT REFERENCES tenants(id),   -- if payable to an agency
  recipient_person_id TEXT REFERENCES persons(id),    -- if payable directly to a person
  amount NUMERIC(14,2) NOT NULL,
  currency TEXT NOT NULL,
  split_reason TEXT NOT NULL                          -- 'listing_side' / 'buyer_side' / 'referral' / 'platform_fee' / 'other'
    CHECK (split_reason IN ('listing_side', 'buyer_side', 'referral', 'platform_fee', 'agent_share_within_tenant', 'other')),
  computed_from JSONB NOT NULL,                       -- rule/agreement snapshot at close time
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'pending_payout'
    CHECK (status IN ('pending_payout', 'paid', 'disputed', 'reversed')),
  paid_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  CHECK ((recipient_tenant_id IS NOT NULL) OR (recipient_person_id IS NOT NULL))
);
```

Split computation happens at close time based on:
- Listing mandate type (exclusive_mandate → higher listing-side share)
- Buyer representation agreement (if exclusive buyer's rep, buyer-side share applies)
- Referral terms if a referral was involved
- Platform fee if applicable
- Intra-tenant agent share (Ali's cut of Elite's commission — governed by Elite's engagement policy)

## 4. Representation model

Relationships between Persons, Agents, Agencies, and Assets that are not covered by tenant membership.

### 4.1 Representation types

```sql
CREATE TABLE representations (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  representation_type TEXT NOT NULL
    CHECK (representation_type IN ('buyer_representation', 'seller_mandate', 'landlord_mandate', 'tenant_representation', 'referral', 'prior_affinity')),
  represented_person_id TEXT REFERENCES persons(id),        -- the party being represented
  representing_tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),  -- the agency doing representation
  representing_agent_id TEXT REFERENCES users(id),          -- the specific agent (may be null for agency-level rep)
  scope JSONB NOT NULL,                                     -- geo / property-type / price range / other constraints
  exclusivity TEXT NOT NULL                                 -- 'exclusive' / 'non_exclusive'
    CHECK (exclusivity IN ('exclusive', 'non_exclusive')),
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'active'
    CHECK (status IN ('proposed', 'active', 'suspended', 'ended', 'expired')),
  agreement_evidence JSONB,                                 -- link to signed representation agreement
  start_date DATE NOT NULL,
  end_date DATE,
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  ended_reason TEXT
);

CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_active_exclusive_buyer_rep
  ON representations(represented_person_id, representation_type)
  WHERE status = 'active' AND exclusivity = 'exclusive'
    AND representation_type IN ('buyer_representation', 'tenant_representation');

CREATE UNIQUE INDEX uq_active_exclusive_listing_mandate
  ON representations(represented_person_id, representation_type)
  WHERE status = 'active' AND exclusivity = 'exclusive'
    AND representation_type IN ('seller_mandate', 'landlord_mandate');
```

### 4.2 Representation influences routing

When a marketplace Lead is generated:

1. Determine `receiving_tenant_id` = primary_listing.tenant_id (§3.7)
2. Query for active exclusive representation of the consumer as buyer (if inquiry is buyer-side)
3. If exclusive buyer rep exists with a DIFFERENT tenant than the receiving tenant:
   - Lead is delivered to receiving tenant per attribution rule (data ownership stays with listing's tenant)
   - Buyer-rep tenant receives a notification with first-right-to-respond within a platform-configured window
   - Listing agent is notified with "buyer has representation" flag
   - After buyer-rep window expires, listing tenant's routing policy engages
4. If non-exclusive representation: platform surfaces choice to the consumer at inquiry time ("You're inquiring on Elite's listing; you have non-exclusive rep with Cedar and personal rep with Ali — who should handle this?")

### 4.3 Representation influences commission

At Transaction close:
- Listing-side commission → to listing's tenant (per listing.representation_type: exclusive_mandate gets full listing share, non-exclusive splits with any other active listing agents)
- Buyer-side commission → to buyer's active exclusive representation tenant, if any (else platform-configured buyer-side handling)
- Referral share → to referral-source tenant per referral agreement
- Platform fee → to platform, per marketplace terms

Split computation is stored in `commission_ledger_entries.computed_from` for full audit.

## 5. Publication vs Ownership — the visibility split

The tenant doc v2's Principle 1 says assets carry `tenant_id`. That remains true for Listings. But **public visibility is orthogonal to ownership**.

### 5.1 The two fields

- `listings.tenant_id` = which agency owns the listing (billing, control, editing rights)
- `listings.publication_status` = whether it appears on the public marketplace feed

### 5.2 Consumer query surface

Public marketplace queries never filter by `tenant_id`. They filter by:
- `publications.publication_state = 'active'`
- Property characteristics matching the search
- Geo filter
- Consumer's saved-search preferences

The consumer sees all published listings from all agencies. This is the whole point of a marketplace.

### 5.3 Agent query surface

Agent dashboard queries filter by `listings.tenant_id IN (my_agencies)` AND `publication_status` per view (draft view, published view, expired view, etc.).

### 5.4 Read authorization

- Anonymous consumer can `GET /api/publications` and `GET /api/publications/:id` without auth
- Agent-scoped reads on `GET /api/listings` require auth + tenant membership per v2 tenant doc
- Cross-agency listing inspection: an agent looking at another agency's listing via the marketplace sees the PUBLIC view (photos, description, price) — not the internal tenant data (commission notes, private annotations)

## 6. Platform-generated leads (marketplace inquiry flow)

The single most-common lead-creation flow: a consumer clicks "Contact Agent" on a Publication.

### 6.1 The flow

1. **Consumer action:** clicks "Contact Agent" on Publication for Property #123.
2. **Identity resolution:**
   - If registered consumer (authenticated): use their `person_id`.
   - If anonymous: platform prompts for phone/email (with GDPR/CCPA-compliant consent). Creates or matches `persons` row. Optionally creates `platform_users` row for future personalization.
3. **Lead creation:** platform creates `marketplace_leads` row with `receiving_tenant_id = primary_listing.tenant_id`. NO agent chooses. Attribution is deterministic.
4. **Representation check:** platform queries for active buyer representation for this consumer.
5. **Routing:** platform applies routing rules:
   - If buyer has exclusive rep with different tenant: buyer's-rep tenant notified with first-right-to-respond
   - Otherwise: listing's tenant's routing policy engages (round-robin, first-response, least-loaded, weighted, manual per tenant doc §11)
6. **Assignment:** specific agent(s) receive the offer per routing strategy. Claim + response SLAs enforced.
7. **Response:** agent responds via chosen channel (WhatsApp/SMS/email/in-platform). Response is recorded against the Lead's conversation thread.

### 6.2 Multiple listings inquiry

If consumer clicks a Publication that aggregates listings from Elite AND Cedar (both listing the same Property non-exclusively):
- One Lead per Listing is created
- Each Lead attributed to its respective agency
- Consumer notified: "Two agencies list this property; we've forwarded your inquiry to both"
- Consumer can see who responds first and decide whom to engage with

### 6.3 Duplicate suppression

If the same consumer inquires on the same property twice within a short window (24h configurable):
- Second inquiry does not create a duplicate Lead
- Second inquiry is appended to the existing conversation as a nudge
- Both parties (consumer and agent) see it as "consumer followed up"

### 6.4 Referral leads

Leads originating from external referral (partner site, external broker) come in via `POST /api/marketplace/leads/referral`:
- Requires referral-partner authentication (API key + tenant identifier)
- `lead_source = 'external_referral'`
- `source_tenant_id` recorded
- Commission arrangement referenced for eventual split

## 7. Cross-tenant transaction lifecycle

The scenario that failed the v2 tenant doc's architectural test, answered end-to-end here.

**Setup:** Nadine (buyer) has exclusive buyer representation with Cedar. Property #123 is listed by Elite (`listing.tenant_id = elite`). Ali is a member of both Elite and Cedar. Elite's listing agent is a different agent (Rana). Nadine communicates via WhatsApp.

### Step-by-step

1. **Listing published:** Elite creates `listings` row with `tenant_id=elite`, `listing_agent_id=rana`, `representation_type=exclusive_mandate`, `publication_status=published`. Platform generates `publications` row aggregating this Listing.

2. **Consumer browses:** Nadine visits the marketplace, sees the Publication for Property #123.

3. **Consumer inquires:** Nadine clicks "Contact Agent". Platform:
   - Resolves Nadine's `person_id` (she's registered).
   - Creates `marketplace_leads` row: `receiving_tenant_id=elite`, `consumer_person_id=nadine`, `primary_listing_id=elite_listing_123`.
   - Detects Nadine's active exclusive buyer rep with Cedar.
   - Notifies Cedar's buyer-rep (may be Ali) with first-right-to-respond (2-hour window default).
   - Ali claims the Lead as Cedar's buyer's rep.
   - Elite's routing policy also engages in parallel; Rana is notified with `buyer_has_representation=true` flag.

4. **Viewing scheduled:** Ali (Cedar buyer's rep) arranges a viewing:
   - `viewings` row: `listing_id=elite_listing_123`, `owning_tenant_id=elite`, `arranging_tenant_id=cedar`, `viewing_agent_id=rana` (Elite's agent shows the property), `consumer_person_id=nadine`.
   - Both Elite and Cedar see the viewing on their calendars (each with appropriate scope).

5. **Offer submitted:** Cedar-side (Ali) submits an offer on Nadine's behalf:
   - `offers` row: `listing_id=elite_listing_123`, `offering_person_id=nadine`, `offering_tenant_id=cedar`, `offer_amount=250000 USD`.
   - Cross-tenant is fine — this is registered as `SUBMIT_OFFER_CROSS_TENANT` in the operation allow-list per v2 §5.
   - Elite (Rana) receives the offer, negotiates with Nadine via Ali.

6. **Negotiation:** counter-offers are created as chained `offers` rows with `parent_offer_id`.

7. **Deal closes:** Elite accepts the final offer. Platform:
   - Creates `marketplace_transactions` row:
     - `listing_id=elite_listing_123`, `accepted_offer_id=<final_offer>`, `buyer_person_id=nadine`, `close_price=245000 USD`
     - `participating_tenant_ids=[elite, cedar]`
     - `participating_agent_ids=[{user_id:rana, role:listing_agent, tenant:elite}, {user_id:ali, role:buyer_rep, tenant:cedar}]`
   - Updates listing: `publication_status=sold`.
   - Aggregated Publication now shows "SOLD" state.

8. **Commission ledger computed:** platform creates `commission_ledger_entries` rows:
   - Listing side (3% of close = 7,350 USD) → recipient_tenant_id=elite, split_reason='listing_side'
   - Buyer side (2.5% of close = 6,125 USD) → recipient_tenant_id=cedar, split_reason='buyer_side'
   - Platform fee (0.5% = 1,225 USD) → recipient_tenant_id=platform, split_reason='platform_fee'
   - Within Elite: Rana's share per Elite's engagement policy for listing agents (say 50%) — subsequent internal entry
   - Within Cedar: Ali's share per Cedar's engagement policy (say 55% for exclusive buyer rep) — subsequent internal entry

9. **Six months later, Ali leaves Cedar:** Ali's Cedar `tenant_memberships` row transitions to `ended`.
   - Historical commission ledger entries retain Ali's participation (Immutability, per v2 §6).
   - Ongoing commission payouts still owed to Ali per the transaction that closed while he was active.
   - Ali retains his personal `person_id` and his personal tenant.
   - Cedar's contact records for Nadine remain with Cedar.
   - Ali may or may not retain access to Nadine's contact per Cedar's departure policy (per v2 §9). If Cedar's policy allows agent-portable contacts for exclusive-buyer-rep clients, Ali takes his personal copy of the contact record.
   - Nadine (as a global `persons` entity) is unaffected; she is not Cedar's property to keep or Ali's property to take. She's a Person on the marketplace.

Every question in the architectural test is answerable:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who owns the buyer? | Nobody. Nadine is a Person on the platform. Cedar has a buyer-rep relationship with her (until ended). |
| Who owns the listing? | Elite (per `listing.tenant_id`). Property #123 itself is platform-level. |
| Who owns the transaction? | Platform. Elite and Cedar are participants. |
| Who owns the conversation? | The Lead's conversation thread is owned by receiving tenant (Elite), with cross-tenant participation record (Cedar buyer-rep). Each side sees their scope. |
| Who owns the WhatsApp account? | The tenant that owns the WhatsApp Business account being used. Elite's Rana replies via Elite's WhatsApp; Cedar's Ali replies via Cedar's WhatsApp. Cross-tenant channel compatibility enforced. |
| Who pays AI costs? | Whichever tenant the AI action is attributed to (based on the asset being acted on and the tenant's engagement policy). |
| Who can see buyer notes? | Cedar's private notes on Nadine are Cedar's only. Elite's private notes are Elite's only. Neither sees the other's. |
| Who sees commission? | Each participating tenant sees its own commission line. Ali sees his intra-tenant share for both Elite (if applicable) and Cedar. Platform sees full ledger. |
| What happens when Ali leaves Cedar? | Membership ends, ongoing commissions still owed per historical record, personal identity retained, Cedar's data retained by Cedar, Ali's departure policy per Cedar's engagement config, no cross-agency data leak. |

**The architecture handles this without exceptions. That's the test passing.**

## 8. TA-in-the-loop approval workflows

Distinct from policy-defined auto-approval (which is silent execution within pre-agreed rules). Some operations require the TA (or an approver they've delegated) to individually approve each occurrence.

### 8.1 Data model

```sql
CREATE TABLE approval_workflow_definitions (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),
  operation_type TEXT NOT NULL,                     -- 'SPEND_CREDITS_ON_LISTING' / 'PUBLISH_LISTING_ABOVE_PRICE' / etc
  threshold_condition JSONB,                        -- when approval required (e.g., {amount_gt: 100})
  approver_selector JSONB NOT NULL,                 -- {role: 'admin'} or {user_ids: [...]} or {external_reviewer_id: ...}
  minimum_approvers INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1,     -- for multi-approver workflows
  sla_hours INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 24,
  default_action_on_sla_expiry TEXT NOT NULL
    CHECK (default_action_on_sla_expiry IN ('auto_approve', 'auto_deny', 'escalate_to_owner')),
  is_active BOOLEAN NOT NULL DEFAULT true,
  created_by_user_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users(id),
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);

CREATE TABLE pending_approvals (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  workflow_definition_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES approval_workflow_definitions(id),
  tenant_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES tenants(id),
  requester_user_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users(id),
  operation_intent JSONB NOT NULL,                  -- full operation payload
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'pending'
    CHECK (status IN ('pending', 'approved', 'denied', 'expired', 'cancelled_by_requester', 'auto_actioned_on_sla')),
  requested_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  sla_deadline TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  approvals JSONB NOT NULL DEFAULT '[]'::jsonb,     -- array of {approver_user_id, action, notes, at}
  final_action_at TIMESTAMPTZ,
  final_action_by_user_id TEXT REFERENCES users(id),
  final_action_notes TEXT
);
```

### 8.2 Runtime flow

1. Agent triggers an operation (e.g., "spend 200 credits on this listing's AI generation").
2. Server evaluates standard authorization (tenant membership, engagement policy, resource compatibility) — all must pass first.
3. Server checks `approval_workflow_definitions` for the operation type in this tenant.
4. If a workflow matches and the threshold is met:
   - Server does NOT execute immediately.
   - Server creates a `pending_approvals` row with the full operation intent.
   - Server notifies approvers per `approver_selector` (in-app notification + email + push per approver's notification preferences).
   - Server returns `202 Accepted` to the agent with `pending_approval_id`.
5. Agent sees "Pending TA approval" state in their UI with option to cancel.
6. Approver(s) see the pending queue in their TA console with full operation context.
7. Approver acts:
   - **Approve** → server executes the operation (as if it had been auto-approved from the start), records approval in ledger.
   - **Deny** → server rejects the operation, notifies agent with approver's notes.
   - **Request info** → sends message back to agent; agent updates operation intent and re-submits.
8. On SLA expiry: server executes `default_action_on_sla_expiry`.

### 8.3 Delegation

TA can delegate approver authority via `approver_selector`:
- `{role: 'admin'}` — any active admin in the tenant can approve
- `{user_ids: [ali_id, sara_id]}` — specific users
- `{external_reviewer_id: legal_counsel_id}` — external reviewer with limited-scope approver access
- Delegation itself is a signed operation (per engagement policy signing infrastructure)

### 8.4 Common approval workflows in real-estate

| Operation | Typical threshold | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Spend AI credits above N | Amount > 100 credits | Prevent runaway agent costs |
| Publish listing above price P | Price > threshold set by TA | Premium-listing review before going live |
| Send bulk campaign to > K recipients | Recipient count > 500 | Prevent WhatsApp Business account bans, brand consistency |
| Post to social publishing account | Any | Brand review before public post |
| Cross-tenant referral | Any referral | Both tenants' TAs approve the referral commercial terms |
| Ownership transfer | Any transfer | Per v2 §6, TRANSFER command requires dual approval |
| Waive tenant policy for a specific action | Any | Explicit exception logging |

### 8.5 UI

- **Requester view:** "Your request to [action] is pending TA approval. Estimated response by [SLA]. [Cancel]"
- **Approver console:** list of pending approvals with full context, requester, deadline, "Approve / Deny / Request info" actions
- **Notification channels:** in-app badge, email, push, optional Slack/Teams webhook

### 8.6 Audit

Every approval decision produces an audit event capturing: requester, action, decision, decider, notes, timestamps, operation intent. Immutable per Principle 10 of the tenant doc.

## 9. Provisional operational state during disputes

Per AI 3's feedback: freezing disputed assets kills marketplace liquidity. Refined disposition workflow:

### 9.1 States

- **`normal`** — asset operates fully
- **`under_review`** — asset operates fully, but marked internally as under-review; commissions escrowed instead of paid, ownership disputed but not yet frozen
- **`provisional_operational`** — asset stays publicly visible under its current agency's brand, but: new leads routed to platform escrow (not either disputing party), commissions escrowed, no editing except by platform admin acting on both parties' consent
- **`frozen`** — no operations except read; used only when both parties agree to freeze or when platform legal is compelled to hold the asset

### 9.2 Default disposition on departure dispute

1. Ali departs Cedar. His Cedar-sourced listings enter `under_review`.
2. Departure workflow runs (per v2 §6.3): agency-owned agency-sourced → to agency, agent-owned personal → to agent, disputed cases → `provisional_operational`.
3. In `provisional_operational`:
   - Public marketplace visibility preserved
   - New leads on these listings routed to a **neutral inbox** managed by platform (or by an agreed neutral party)
   - Leads held for both parties' visibility with a "dispute in progress" tag
   - Any commission that closes during this state is held in escrow
4. Both parties document their claims via platform-provided case-record page.
5. Resolution is off-platform (per v2 §9). Both parties sign resolution acknowledgment via Ed25519.
6. Platform executes the agreed resolution: TRANSFER to winning party, release escrowed commissions per agreed split, close case.
7. Only if parties cannot agree AND legal compulsion arises does the asset move to `frozen`.

### 9.3 Preserving marketplace integrity

Consumers should not see disputed-listing badges publicly (bad UX, may deter inquiries). The visibility to the consumer is unchanged; the operational state is internal.

## 10. Ownership event ledger (event-sourced)

Per AI 1's feedback: current-owner-plus-history isn't as rich as event-sourced.

### 10.1 Event types

Ledger records events, not just states:

- `ASSET_CREATED`
- `ASSET_ATTRIBUTION_ASSIGNED` (creation-time tenant assignment)
- `ASSET_TRANSFERRED` (TRANSFER command executed)
- `ASSET_ASSIGNED_TO_AGENT` (intra-tenant assignment)
- `ASSET_RELEASED_FROM_AGENT` (intra-tenant release)
- `ASSET_DELEGATED_TO_TENANT` (responsibility delegation without ownership transfer)
- `ASSET_REVOKED_DELEGATION`
- `ASSET_ENTERED_DISPUTE`
- `ASSET_DISPUTE_RESOLVED`
- `ASSET_ARCHIVED`
- `ASSET_RESTORED`

### 10.2 Schema

```sql
CREATE TABLE asset_ownership_events (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  asset_type TEXT NOT NULL,
  asset_id TEXT NOT NULL,
  event_type TEXT NOT NULL,
  from_tenant_id TEXT REFERENCES tenants(id),
  to_tenant_id TEXT REFERENCES tenants(id),
  from_agent_id TEXT REFERENCES users(id),
  to_agent_id TEXT REFERENCES users(id),
  performed_by_user_id TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES users(id),
  performed_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP,
  authorization_basis TEXT,
  reason TEXT,
  approvers JSONB,                          -- for approval-required events
  operation_intent_snapshot JSONB,          -- what was requested
  asset_state_snapshot JSONB,               -- key fields at event time
  parent_event_id TEXT REFERENCES asset_ownership_events(id),  -- for related event chains
  created_at TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL DEFAULT CURRENT_TIMESTAMP
);
CREATE INDEX idx_asset_ownership_events_asset ON asset_ownership_events(asset_type, asset_id, performed_at);
```

Append-only. No updates, no deletes. The full lineage of any asset's ownership + agent assignment + delegation history is reconstructable.

Current owner: derived from latest `ASSET_ATTRIBUTION_ASSIGNED` or `ASSET_TRANSFERRED` event. Kept denormalized on the asset row (`asset.tenant_id`) for query performance, but the event log is authoritative.

## 11. Explicit / inherited / no-attribution creation states

Per AI 1: creation-time attribution has three distinct states that should be recorded.

- **`explicit_user_selection`** — user actively chose the target tenant (clicked a card in Pattern A, or opened the Pattern B dropdown and selected)
- **`inherited_from_parent`** — creation was launched from a parent asset that had a tenant; the parent's tenant was pre-selected and the user did NOT change it
- **`system_assigned`** — platform assigned tenant (marketplace-generated leads, referrals, migration backfills)

Recorded on the created asset as `tenant_assignment_source`. Used for:
- Audit forensics ("was this deliberately assigned to Elite or inherited from a parent?")
- Debugging attribution disputes
- UX display ("This lead was auto-attributed to Elite because it came from an Elite listing")

## 12. Cross-marketplace operations & marketplace as first-class participant

The platform itself acts in the marketplace in several ways:

- **Marketplace fees** — platform is a commission recipient on transactions per marketplace terms
- **Marketplace-generated leads** — platform is the LEAD CREATOR, not any agency
- **Marketplace listings from external partners** — platform is the ingest-source for external-partner listings (Property Finder, MLS, etc.)
- **Marketplace moderation** — platform can hold listings in `publication_state = 'moderation_hold'` for content review
- **Marketplace analytics** — platform generates marketplace-wide analytics that require cross-tenant aggregation (with tenant-sensitive data excluded unless separately authorized)
- **Marketplace disputes** — platform provides the dispute-preservation infrastructure (§9), never adjudicates

The platform's role must be modeled as a first-class participant, not smuggled in via "system" or "admin" flags. Platform actions carry their own audit context and are visible in relevant timelines.

## 13. Consumer lifecycle and rights

### 13.1 Lifecycle states

- **Anonymous browser** — session cookie (with consent), no `platform_users` row, no `persons` row
- **Registered consumer** — `platform_users` row, `persons` row, verified email or phone
- **Active consumer** — has recent activity (inquiries, saved searches, viewings)
- **Dormant consumer** — no activity in configurable period (default 12 months)
- **Suspended** — platform-suspended for TOS violation
- **Closed** — user closed their account; retained per legal requirements

### 13.2 Rights (per Lebanon E-Transactions Law + GDPR + platform terms)

- **Right of access:** consumer can export all their marketplace activity + all `persons` linkages
- **Right to erasure:** field-level retention semantics apply:
  - Marketing-preference data: erasable
  - Search/browsing history: erasable
  - Transaction records: legally retained per Lebanese financial and real-estate law (minimum retention period)
  - Contact records held by agencies: agencies notified; each responds per its retention obligations
- **Right to portability:** structured data export in JSON/CSV
- **Right to rectification:** consumer can correct their `persons` identifiers via verification flow

### 13.3 Consumer consent management

- Cookie consent captured at first visit
- Communication consent captured at inquiry submission (email, phone, WhatsApp)
- Marketing consent captured separately (opt-in)
- Data sharing consent captured for cross-tenant identity linking (per tenant doc §7)
- All consents stored with timestamps, IP, mechanism (checkbox / OTP / signed)
